# DRAFT for owner approval: the rubric and brief rewritten for a crafted figure, not a real child

Nothing here has been used on an auditor yet. The old rubric and brief stay as they are until you approve this.

## What changes, in one line
The figure is judged as a well-made automaton of a child, openly a made object. Realism of the face is no longer scored. Craft, detail, thematic fit and proportion are.

## THE PIECE (replacement paragraph 1)
"Slots" is one of the landing-intro games of Daily Roll Over, a casino web app played on phones. It runs in WebGL in the app's dark casino room. It shows a child automaton of the 1770s, after Jaquet-Droz's "Writer", and it is openly a made object: a crafted figure, not a real child, and it must not be judged on how well it passes for one. ref/writer-real.png is the real automaton and shows the kind of object it should be. ref/owner-target.png is the owner's colour and costume target only: a dark "money green" velvet overcoat, brown curls, a warm child's face. The figure is meant to be a beautifully made character figure: a child's head with warm, finished skin, a child's soft proportions and dark brown eyes, a mop of loose dark-brown ringlets under a black felt tricorn with gold braid, a gold hatband and a cream plume (the owner asked for the hat and likes it), a bottle-green velvet overcoat with a darker waistcoat and cuffs, and lace. He sits at a walnut-and-gilt writing desk on a dais in front of oxblood velvet drapes, and three slot reels turn in a glass window in his chest (the owner chose to keep the reels in his chest). Judge how well each part is made and how well the parts belong together, not the choices themselves.

## THE BAR (unchanged)
The reference sheet shows the app's R×R card-table home. Treat it as the 7/10 bar.

## RUBRIC (replacement)
Score 1-10, half points allowed, on these checks:
- **detail:** every edge finished (trim, braid, buttons, lace, hair ends, lids), at the three distances: silhouette across the room, materials and seams at arm's length, and detail at a 3x pinch
- **theme:** every part belongs to one story (the era, the costume, the desk, the lever, the reels); nothing looks borrowed from another set
- **proportion:** a strong silhouette; features and body sized by intent, as a craftsperson would size them; a child's proportions read without needing realism
- light: what each surface does with the light
- contact: things touch, and shadows land
- motion with weight
- readability: titles, captions and overlays
- whether each tier's moment reads

Do not penalise the figure for being a figure. Do penalise any part that looks generated, unfinished, accidental or inconsistent with the rest: shard-like or striped hair, flat painted-on features that do not match the finish elsewhere, parts that float, poke through or clip, and materials that read as flat colour.

The scale:
- 10: a collectible-quality crafted figure. Every part finished, consistent and intended, at every distance and frame.
- 9: polished. A viewer can't point at an unfinished part without pinching in.
- 7: meets the R×R card-table bar. Credible and shippable, with a few unfinished parts visible at arm's length.
- 5: reads as intended, but parts look generated rather than made (flat, shard-like, unfinished, inconsistent) in places.
- 3: broken or confusing.

## Calibration (new, one extra step)
Before the first scored round, the same auditor scores ref/writer-real.png on the same rubric, blind. If the real Writer does not score 8 or higher, the rubric is still asking for something a made object cannot be, and it is revised before any build is scored.

## What the re-baseline would be
One auditor on the current build (v141) under this rubric, plus the Writer calibration, and nothing else. The score it gives is the new starting point. The old 4.5 stays on record as the realism-rubric plateau and is not compared with it.

## What stays the same
The closed issue-id list, the pairs, the idea bank and the rest of the pipeline. The FACE-FORM id is re-read as "head, jaw, chin, cheek shape and proportion", with doll-like no longer a defect on its own.
